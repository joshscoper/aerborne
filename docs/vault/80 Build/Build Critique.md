# Build Critique

Start with the player's task and the view in the build sheet. Ask a fresh player to reach the destination with ordinary movement. Watch where they stop or turn back before explaining the design. A missed doorway is more actionable feedback than “add detail.”

## Keep a repeatable set of views

| View | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Distant skyline from the normal approach | Which mass identifies this place before its blocks resolve? |
| Eye-level shot at the last decision point | Can you point to the destination and the path leading to it? |
| Oblique view across the threshold | Do the foundation, jamb, roof and slope meet? Can the player enter from this angle? |
| Interior facing the exit, then the reverse walk | Can the player use the space and find the way back? |
| Same junction at night | Where does the eye go first, and can a player see changes of level? |

For before and after, record revision, world, XYZ, yaw, pitch, FOV, pack, time, weather and render distance. Capture from player height for the walk. Mark aerials, offline plans, reference art and client screenshots by type. If only an overhead plan exists, leave visual review pending for the other views.

## Diagnose before changing blocks

| Symptom | Check first | Smallest informative revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spectacular skyline, confusing street | Stand at the turn where the landmark vanishes. | Shift the obstructing roof edge or open a framed view through the corner. Repeat the walk from both sides. |
| A city reads as a row of houses | Trace one delivery and one civic route. Which roof and entrance serve each? | Give one frontage a functional change such as a hoist yard, arcade or processional stair. Recheck the whole street in silhouette. |
| Road looks painted on | Cut a section at its steepest approach. | Tie the deck into grade, expose the retaining face and connect the verge or drain. Test the landing on foot. |
| Wall looks busy but flat | Cover the texture in a screenshot or inspect a plain-block bay. | Recess the opening and carry the eave on a visible support, then restore one patch of wear. |
| Night shot looks good but players miss the exit | View the room from the occupied position, including the boss or NPC. | Move a light pool to the threshold and cut back the foreground obstruction. Repeat during actual play. |

## Decide the next pass

- **Revise layout** if the route fails, the hero disappears at its intended reveal, the terrain enters a room, or an encounter cannot be played as designed. Reopen the plan and section.
- **Revise finish** when the walk and masses work but a material transition, empty eye-level band, repeated kit or lighting cue reads poorly. Change one cause at a time and retake the matching view.
- **Ready for human review** when the critical walk, forward and reverse views, and intended interactions have been checked on the candidate revision. Send the evidence and remaining weaknesses. Record the user's verdict separately.
- **Pending** if an essential view, playtest or verdict is missing. Say exactly what is available, who inspected it and what remains to be checked.

## Turn critique into a reusable lesson

The [[Build Audit 2026-11-04]] documents a real failure worth retaining. Hillsides ran through houses whose floors had been set independently of the uphill ground. The remedy was a grading ring, retaining face on the cut side and supported plinth on the fill side. The geometry test guards the footprint. A future builder should also walk the *approach* and inspect the uphill doorway; a valid floor alone cannot prove that the road connects to it.

Use [[Review Record Template]] for a new lesson. State the triggering condition, visible symptom, change, evidence and where the remedy breaks down. On the next comparable project, link back to that record and try the lesson again. Keep conflicting evidence beside the original so later builders can see when a rule applies.
