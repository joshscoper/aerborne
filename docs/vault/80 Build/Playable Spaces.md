# Playable Spaces

Plan a room around what players do there. A flour mill needs a delivery door, a path past the stones to the store and a way for a customer to reach the miller. A combat room needs a readable threat, space to sidestep, cover appropriate to the attack and an exit. Sketch these paths inside the walls before furnishing the room.

## A cellar encounter in plain blocks

For [[Open-World Dungeons]], try this prototype under the Black Granary. Its dimensions are starting values for testing, not the as-built room sizes.

| Beat | Geometry and cue | Playtest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Descent | Three-wide stair enters under a heavy lintel. Reveal a lit slice of the first arch before the last turn. | Walk down and back without jumping or clipping your head. Can two players pass? |
| Long cellar | Block a 9 × 17 clear hall with three arch bays. Put a cantor beyond the first bay and cover at the side of the second. | One player enters while another retreats. Can both read the caster and find room to break its line of sight? |
| Discovery | A split stack of sacks reveals a sliver of a side passage. Its entrance is visible on the return angle as well. | Walk past without diverting, then take the branch. The side reward should bring the player back to the same legible hall. |
| Boss threshold | Frame the throne beneath a high opening. Leave open floor between the entrance and throne, and two paths around a broken support. | Place the actual attack footprint. Can a player see its boundary and reach a safe position while another player occupies the near lane? |
| Return | Put the grain chute beside the reward and bring it to a recognizable exterior marker. | Starting at the chest, reach open ground and identify the route back to the hub. |

Resize the room to the real mob, telegraph, party size and movement speed. Reserve the encounter volumes for combat first. Add posts, ribs and props outside those volumes and repeat the playtest with final collision. A boss alcove can be dramatic and still leave a clear floor for a dodge.

## The ordinary rooms matter too

In a shop, stand at the player's side of the counter and check sight and interaction with the NPC. Enter from both directions with the door swung open. Trace a delivery route to storage. A busy counter can support a shelf, till and goods, while the accessible edge stays clean.

At a route junction, face each exit from the standing point where the decision occurs. Distinguish each by destination: the mill wheel seen through an arch, the open field beyond a hedge gap, or a lit stair back to town. A sign can supply a name after the space has made the choice understandable.

Record gameplay checks separately from visual judgement in [[Build Critique]]. A wall that provides excellent cover may hide the boss portrait, and the camera that sells the throne may hide a blocked exit.
