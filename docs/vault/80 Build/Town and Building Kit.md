# Town and Building Kit

## Lay out a working district

Trace a working day. At a dock, a load travels from ship to hoist to warehouse to counting room to cart street. Put those spaces in sequence, including the turn a cart needs at the warehouse. A visitor takes a different path past the posting board to the inn. Their crossing makes a credible place for a small square and a lamp. Use the terrain to decide which path passes above the other.

Give each street a destination and each ward a dominant roof family. Reuse the bay width, eave treatment and footing. Vary the building's use, footprint, ridge direction and entrance. A long warehouse opens at cart height; a smithy turns a covered work yard toward the road; an inn needs both a public door and a route for deliveries. This variation changes the silhouette even in a single material.

## Build one timber hall before repeating it

Prototype dimensions below are in blocks. Count footprint dimensions including both end walls. Heights are relative to the top of the finished floor.

| Component | First pass | Inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site | Stake a 9 × 13 shell and a one-block inspection ring. Sample corners and door approach. Cut the uphill edge and fill the downhill side to a faced plinth. | Walk the entire outside ring. The uphill door must open and the downhill corner must meet ground. |
| Frame | Place corner posts and posts at four-block intervals along the 13-block side (positions 0, 4, 8, 12). Tie them with a plate at height 5. | From the street, posts carry beam ends and the lower sill follows the plinth. |
| Wall | Set plaster one block behind the front posts. Use one bay for a recessed entrance and the next for a paired window. Plan the usable room width after the recess. | Stand one bay to the side. The return reveals actual depth. Look through the window from inside. |
| Roof | Run the ridge along the long axis. Over the 9-wide gable, use symmetric stair courses rising inward to a single center ridge. Extend the eave one block past each side, add end overhangs and seat the underside on brackets. | Check both gables in profile and the eave from below. Keep the ridge continuous through the main volume. |
| Occupancy | Place hearth and chimney together at an end wall. Reserve a path from entrance to counter and a second route from counter to storage. Cut upper floor openings before placing stairs. | Walk the public and work routes with the doors open. Inspect the ceiling above each stair tread. |
| Extension | Attach a lower lean-to at the work yard; carry its outer beam on posts. Leave daylight between the lower roof edge and the main windows. | The smaller roof explains a use and gives the main hall a readable outline. |

For a masonry district, retain the bay rhythm but replace timber posts with piers. Spring arches from the same height on both sides; thicken the piers beside larger openings. For a stepped settlement, stitch floor levels together with landings. Place doors on the landings and give the retaining wall a coping. A stair facing a shut wall gives the street away immediately.

## Scale up from hall to landmark

Draw the skyline as a solid silhouette. Choose a broad base, a tall principal mass and a lower attached volume. Leave sky between important peaks. On a capital gate, carry the bridge or avenue between the towers, frame the arch with piers and set the gate mechanism into a volume deep enough to contain it. Follow the load down through each crossing to a wall or abutment.

Walk toward it at three distances. From the horizon identify the tower pair; from the street find the opening; at the threshold see where to go next. If the towers merge with workshops in the middle shot, change their overlap or lower an intervening ridge. A taller flag helps only at the far distance. [[Reading a Build Reference]] shows the Kingsgate approach.

**Variation test.** Place three related buildings side by side in plain blocks. Assign a use to each, then hide their signs. If the loading bay, forge yard and inn porch still identify their buildings, record the kit choices worth repeating. If the houses all read as the same cottage, change the plan or roof before changing the wall palette.
