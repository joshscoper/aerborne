# Landform and Routes

## Draw two maps of the same ground

On the overhead plan mark the arrival, destination, watercourse, high ground and return loop. Trace the journey of a player and the journey of supplies. A quarry can sit above its loading yard, but the loaded cart needs a graded path to the road. Where the paths cross, a bridge, market or gate has a reason to exist. Give each zone a distant identifying shape and a boundary the player can recognize on foot.

Draw a vertical section along the hardest part of the walk. Mark street surface, door threshold, waterline, bridge deck and hill crest using one height convention. For example, a street that climbs six blocks to a gate needs six one-block rises, additional room for landings and enough headroom under every crossing. Move the gate back if the rise eats the plaza. A level map can conceal that conflict.

## Shape a valley in four passes

1. Lay a continuous thalweg (the lowest line of the valley) from source to outlet. Broaden the floor at a lake or floodplain, narrow it at a rock pinch. In the Reach, carry the outlet over the island lip. Inspect the full course for uphill stretches and dry gaps.
2. Cut asymmetrical banks. On one bend make a steep outside bank and an inside gravel shelf. Lead the road to a narrow stable crossing, start the deck on dry abutments and grade the approaches before decorating the water.
3. Establish a ridge with a shoulder and two subsidiary spurs. Cut smaller gullies down from the spurs. Leave flatter shelves for farms. From the main road, at least one gap between spurs should reveal the destination.
4. Paint by exposure. Put exposed rock on the steep faces, soil on shelves, damp growth in sheltered low ground. Blend a forest into fields with a broken band of scrub and isolated trees. Carry one material across the border as the other changes.

**A floating landmass needs a second silhouette.** Sketch the side as well as the plan. Build a thick root of rock under a heavy district, recess the adjacent face and taper a spur over the void. Continue a visible stratum around the return face. Inspect from the opposite bank and from skiff height. If the island reads as a flat slab with spikes, change the underside volumes before adding vines.

## Give a forest depth

Build one tree in the clear. Start with buttress roots that meet the slope, taper the trunk, fork at different heights and place leaf clusters on branch tips. Leave gaps that reveal the sky. For a spruce, keep the leader taller than the upper branches and let the lower tier reach farther. For an orchard tree, start the fork low, lean two arms over the picking lane and leave a clear profile beneath them.

Place trees in stands. Vary height within each stand, graduate from tall trees in the interior to saplings and shrubs at the edge, then leave a clearing long enough to see the next landmark. For a generator, select variants from stable world coordinates and cap their extent at roads, doors, water and reserved views. Walk beside the stand and inspect its reverse silhouette. If branches read as identical green balls, vary branch direction, cluster size and the gaps between clusters.

## Engineer the road

Sketch the cross-section before the path material. A country lane might have a five-block usable surface, a one-block verge on each side and a ditch on the uphill side. At a steep cut, expose a retaining face. At a drop, carry the outer edge on fill or an arch. Run drains downhill with an outlet. At a bridge, bring the road surface flush to the deck and keep a clear sightline past the first pier.

Use the stable two-abreast, three-high route target from [[Build Style Guide]] for the Reach. Wider streets need a reason for the extra width, such as cart turning or a market queue. Mark intersections with a difference in paving, an opening in the hedge or a framed view. Walk the return route too. A mill that guides players on arrival may sit behind them on the way home.

**Field exercise.** Block out two valley crossings with the same deck, one spanning a wide floodplain and one at the pinch. Walk each from the last bend. Choose using approach grade, foundation depth and the moment the destination appears. Record the trade-off in [[Build Sheet Template]].
