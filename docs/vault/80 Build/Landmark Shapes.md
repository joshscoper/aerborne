# Landmark Shapes

A distant landmark needs an identifiable outline from at least two directions. A tree can frame a mast at the docks and swallow it completely from the bridge. Before texturing, draw a front, side and overhead shape, then reserve the gaps that make the subject readable.

## Prototype a crane over a sky dock

Use local X for the reach over the quay, Y for height above deck and Z for depth. The dimensions here describe a prototype. Refit it to the ship and lifting clearance on the actual site.

1. Stake a six-by-six base. Place two timber uprights spanning four blocks of depth and tie them into a stone or piled foundation. Keep the cart lane on the landward side.
2. From the pivot near local (0, 9, 0), sketch a boom ending at (12, 13, 0). Build one rising course per three blocks of reach. Under it, run a diagonal brace from low on the tower to the outer half of the boom. Seen from the water, the two members should enclose a visible triangle.
3. Drop the chain from the outer tip toward a load on the pier. Give the pivot a short landward counterweight and a winch at operating height. Trace the load path from chain to boom to brace to foundation. If the cargo would swing through an awning, move the hoist or its receiving bay.
4. Inspect from the pier and from the ship's approach. End-on, the boom may disappear behind its mast. Rotate the whole rig or move the viewing route until there is air between chain and tower. Use two states across a harbor, such as a crane parked above a stack and another lifting a net. Preserve the same structural family.

## Sculpt a figure that reads across a bridge

Set the pose with lines for spine, shoulders, bent arm and staff. Test in solid blocks with one light material. Allow a gap between arm and torso, and between staff and leg. Compare the pose from the foot of the plinth, across the water and from the reverse side. If the staff merges with the body, change the gesture before carving a face. Widen a thin diagonal where the voxel stair-step vanishes at the intended distance.

Build the plinth wide enough to support the stance, give its cap a visible lip and keep the inscription at walking height. Reserve fine carving for the side a visitor can actually approach. Use [[Highcrown]] for the purpose of the Walk of Wardens and differentiate figures by posture or carried object so they work in silhouette.

## Make curves survive the block grid

For a round tower, plot one quarter of the outer ring and mirror the run lengths to the other quadrants. Inspect from a diagonal for long flat sides. Thicken the ring inward and keep its outer silhouette fixed as you hollow the floor. For a taper, stack smaller rings with a few vertical courses between setbacks; inspect the side profile for a sudden pinched neck. Use stairs, slabs or wall blocks on the final edge where their collision and orientation suit the surface.

For an organic root or rib, plot a centerline in both plan and elevation. Place thick connected cross-sections perpendicular to the main direction, taper toward the tip, then cut small inside corners to expose the bend. Walk underneath. Branches should join the parent volume and leave usable clearance over the route. These same sections can form a ship's hull, a cavern arch or the foot of a colossal statue.

**Review.** Reduce a normal in-game shot to thumbnail size. Describe the landmark using only its outline, one gap and its relation to the route. If those three cues identify it, return to full size to check material transitions, supports and the view from directly below.
