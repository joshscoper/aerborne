# World Composition

Design the world at the distances people actually experience. The map must identify regions, the horizon must announce where the player is going, and the ground must explain the next turn. A pleasing outline from above can still produce a ten-minute walk between indistinguishable hills.

## Make three drawings before filling the map

1. **Region silhouettes.** On the same sheet sketch each region as a side profile and a small patch of colour. Try the profiles in one neutral colour first. In [[Goldenreach Vale]], terrace steps and windmill sails can identify the region without the wheat palette. In [[Ironspine Highlands]], a high broken ridge and a cut through it promise a different kind of walk.
2. **Connected plan.** Put the arrival, settlement, working land, danger, dungeon entrance and onward route on the map. Draw walking edges between them and identify each crossing. Add one secondary loop that rejoins the spine at a recognizable place. Show where rivers, cliffs and island gaps allow a view but require a detour. Respect the guidance-only level gating in [[Decisions]].
3. **Journey section.** Along one important route, draw height against distance. Mark when each hero first appears, where it disappears and where the player uses it to make a decision. Check both directions. If the landmark disappears exactly at the fork, move the fork, cut a notch in the ridge or provide a closer marker with the same identity.

Use the local overview art and level ladder in [[World Overview]]. On a fresh world, choose travel times from the brief, then measure walking time on the blockout with the intended movement mode. Include actual stairs, encounters and forks. Revise empty stretches before placing more POIs.

## Worked sequence for a Reach slice

| Leg | What players should read | Transition to prototype |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highcrown docks → upper ward | The city climbs from freight to commerce to civic buildings. | Frame the next stair from the previous landing. Open a side view over the piers so progress in height is visible. |
| Kingsgate → Vale | Closely spaced roof peaks give way to terraces and open sky. | Carry the gate's stone into a road abutment, fade to field walls, then allow a long open view over the first crops. |
| Fields → Millbrook | A working settlement gathers near water. | Put the lane, bridge approach and receiving yards on one grade drawing. Give the mill a profile that survives the last bend. |
| Millbrook → Black Granary | Familiar grain storage becomes the dark landmark on higher ground. | Repeat the grain architecture in altered condition. Use burned roof gaps and an exposed tower crown to identify the change at a distance. |
| Granary → road onward | The player emerges with a new direction and a familiar point behind them. | Frame a landmark on the next leg and preserve a look-back to the Vale. Test the return without quest markers. |

This is a proposed reading of the route. Confirm against the generated geography and zone notes. Boundary transitions need depth: show the approaching region through a gap, let one landform or material cross the border, then put the first decisive change where the player can see it. Reserve an occasional quiet stretch so a major reveal has room to matter.

## Expand only after a seam works

Finish one corridor from a hub, across a region seam, to a recognizable destination. Split further work by visible catchments such as a valley, quay or ridge face. For each adjoining parcel record the shared road centerline, surface heights, waterline, palette overlap and protected views. Build and walk both sides of the seam together.

From a high overview, trace every primary route with a finger. From the route, ask a player to name the next destination at its last junction. From the far shore, check the underside, skyline and negative space between islands. If all three views identify the right place, save them as baselines before populating the next district.
