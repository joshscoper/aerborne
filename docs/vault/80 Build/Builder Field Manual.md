# Builder Field Manual

Start a build by naming the walk a player will remember. “A courier comes through the gate and recognizes the granary by its burned crown across the wheat” gives you a route, a view, a silhouette and a reason to build. “Build a detailed medieval zone” leaves all four undecided.

## Work in passes

1. **Brief.** Choose a player action, the principal view, and two references with different jobs. One can explain construction and the other composition. Extract one testable relationship from each. Use [[Reading a Build Reference]].
2. **Plan.** Draw the route in plan and section. Put the destination, turns, grade changes, doors and occupied floors on the same drawing. Mark what must stay empty. See [[Millbrook Terrace Study]] for a filled example.
3. **Blockout.** Build the ground, route and three largest masses in plain blocks. Walk forward and back at player height. When a roof hides the destination, change its height, location or the approach before choosing materials.
4. **Finish one slice.** Take a single approach, doorway and room through grading, structure, landscaping, lighting and gameplay. Record the palette, dimensions and variation that worked. Expand outward only when the route and view work together.
5. **Review and revise.** Use [[Build Critique]] at the same cameras before and after the change. Record the strongest remaining fault and its evidence. Carry a lesson into the next build with [[Review Record Template]].

## Starting a Codex or Claude session

> Read Builder Field Manual and the notes it selects for this task. Use the project brief and latest zone note. Give me the arrival view, a dimensioned plan and section, one palette sample, and the first playable slice. For each design choice name the player view or action that will test it. Separate observed conditions from proposed dimensions. At review, show the before and after from the same camera and say what still needs an in-world check.
