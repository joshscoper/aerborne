# Surface and Light

## Make a sample that includes a corner

Build a seven-wide, five-high wall panel with a three-block return, an opening, a footing and an eave. Use the target pack and biome. View the sample from one block away, from across the road, and in shade. A flat palette strip hides what happens when light strikes a return or the roof casts a shadow.

For a Goldenreach terrace, try a cobblestone retaining face, stone-brick coping and mossy cobblestone around a drain outlet. Keep the middle stretch mostly one stone. Use the brighter mortar lines to trace the coping, with warm wood for the gate. Reserve a red cloth accent for the occupied farm door. Select the final blocks from the rendered sample; the same block can change apparent value beside wheat or in a shaded recess.

Give each variation a cause. Put a rain streak below a sill, exposed stone on a worn corner, moss where water leaves the wall and smooth replacement masonry around a repaired lintel. Continue the mark around the corner if the water would travel there. For generated work, use contiguous patches guided by surface and moisture; per-block random replacement creates confetti at walking distance. See [[Reading a Build Reference]] for the WesterosCraft exercise.

## Decide what gets detail

| Distance | Spend detail on | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Across the valley | Roof silhouette, field bands, cliff profile and the brightest broad field of colour | At thumbnail size, can the viewer point to the landmark? |
| Across the street | Wall returns, window recesses, braced eaves and occupied balconies | From the side, can the viewer tell how the roof meets the wall? |
| At the door | Handle, lettered sign, threshold wear, workbench and delivery props | Can a player enter, trade and turn around without snagging? |

Tie props to an action. At a cooper's shop, stack hoop stock under the lean-to, put a half-built barrel beside the bench and finished barrels at the handcart. Keep the public threshold clear. Place the richest cluster where the player slows or turns; let the facing wall rest so the work area stands out.

## Light the walk

Start in ordinary game lighting. Put the first visible light at the active door, a second at the stair landing and a softer pool near the next junction. Show a plausible fixture or opening for each lit area. Leave unoccupied roof slopes relatively quiet. In a cave, rough in a skylight shaft or exposed crystal seam and inspect where the light lands before placing accent sources.

Repeat the walk at night, with the same resource pack, FOV, brightness and render distance. From the turn, can the player distinguish a usable stair from a recess? From the landing, can they see the next safe step? If the square glows evenly, lower or remove competing lights and concentrate illumination at the route decision. If a dark drop is dangerous, light its edge and fix its geometry. Use the shader-free baseline for acceptance; record enhanced shots separately.

**Repair order for a noisy wall.** Remove isolated contrast blocks, restore a continuous area of the base material, give the footing and opening depth, then reintroduce one weathered patch at its source. Compare against the original from the opposite pavement.
