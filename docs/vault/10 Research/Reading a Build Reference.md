# Reading a Build Reference

Pick the actual viewing position. A skyline seen from a skiff, a street seen on foot and a cutaway of a dungeon answer different questions. For each image record the source, what you can see, one relationship you can transfer, and the shot that could disprove your design. Borrow spatial relationships and develop original shapes, symbols and layouts.

## Two readings from the local collection

![[ref-04-city-gate-street.webp|480]]

**What is visible.** Near roof edges enter both top corners. Lower stall awnings overlap the side buildings, while a continuous strip of paving reaches the opening between the gate towers. A patch of brighter space is visible *through* the opening. The emblem occupies the panel above it. Cover the emblem and the route still reads.

**Transfer to Highcrown.** From Market Avenue, frame Kingsgate with the two nearest stall roofs. Reserve an uninterrupted walking band and a clear view through the arch. Keep a patch of sky between the towers and any competing spire. Take one shot from the middle and one from the stall side where a shopper actually approaches. If a valance cuts across the opening in the second view, shorten or shift that stall before making the gate taller. See [[Highcrown]].

![[ref-07-wheat-terraces.webp|480]]

**What is visible.** Wheat occupies much of the foreground in stepping, uneven strips. A path climbs at the left. The village fills the middle distance above short changes in grade. Tall trunks pass in front of the roofs, but gaps between trunks reveal lit wall panels and awnings. This is a layered view even without counting the individual blocks.

**Transfer to Goldenreach.** Give the traveller a near field edge, a farm door one terrace higher, and a clump of trees behind the roofs. Keep a gap for the door as the player rounds the field bend. In a monochrome blockout the steps should overlap and the farm should still have an approach. [[Millbrook Terrace Study]] develops this into a buildable section.

The originals live locally in the vault's `_refs/` folder. If the embeds are unavailable, use the written observations and [[Reference Images]]. Record that limitation on the build sheet.

## Techniques worth studying at their source

- **WesterosCraft, facade gradients.** The team's illustrated example orders blocks by brightness, undertone and texture, then puts damage where water gathers. Exercise: build the same bay twice, once with random block variation and once with a dark cluster below a sill. Compare from across the street and retain the one that reads as damp masonry. Use equivalent blocks from the target pack. [WesterosCraft gradient guide](https://westeroscraft.com/docs/building/fundamentals/gradients)
- **ArdaCraft, settlement planning.** Its application asks builders to plan the outskirts and show test builds for specific parts of a project. Exercise: trace grain from the field gate to the mill door to a loading yard. Reserve each use on the plot before laying out the houses. [ArdaCraft project application](https://www.ardacraft.me/resources/project-application)
- **The Level Design Book, playable blockouts.** Its blockout process tests scale and movement while the layout is cheap to change. Exercise: give a fresh player the task “reach the mill and return to the road.” Record the first wrong turn and what the player could see there. [Blockout](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout) · [Wayfinding](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/wayfinding)

For further style studies see [[Shovel Study]], [[Stormwind Study]] and [[Builders and Terraformers]]. For any new study, preserve the distinction between the artist's observable work and the rule you propose to test.
