# WoW mob design

## Sourced
- **Elite areas were open-world group content.** In classic WoW, whole outdoor areas were elite and worked as
  non-instanced group content. Patch 2.3 turned many to normal mobs, and outdoor elite areas faded out. *(S8)*
- **Outdoor elites ≈ +30% health and damage** over a normal mob of their level is a working rule of thumb; rare
  elites and dungeon elites are much stronger. *(S8)*
- **Rares** respawn far less often (some take hours), are usually no tougher than normal mobs of their level, and
  exist to be found. *(S8)*
- **Too many rares** cheapens them: Blizzard cut rare elites in two late zones because the map showed 12–15 at
  once. *(S9)*
- **Creature spacing matters.** Fighting many different creatures in a row breaks pacing (Kaplan). *(S1)*

## How a WoW zone's mobs are organised (play)
- **Families, not lists.** A zone has three to five families:
  1. **Wildlife** native to the place (boars, wolves, spiders, crabs). Neutral or easily pulled.
  2. **A local hostile people** (bandits, gnolls, murlocs), living in **camps**: tents, a fire, a banner, 4–8 mobs
     standing in small groups, one **caster** at the back, sometimes a named leader in the middle.
  3. **The story's enemy**, the threat behind the zone's mystery (the Defias in Westfall). Shows up more as the
     story goes on and owns the dungeon.
  4. **An oddity** that gives the zone its flavour (Westfall's harvest golems).
- **Placement by spot.** Wildlife roams open land; people stay in camps; undead stay in graveyards and ruins.
  Players learn to read the land: smoke means a camp.
- **Patrols** walk roads and between camps. A pair of patrollers on a road teaches "look before you pull".
- **Levels rise away from the hub.** The first field outside town is the lowest; the far side of the zone is the
  top of its band.
- **Named mobs** stand at the heart of a camp (quest kill), in a dungeon (boss) or roam (rare).
- **Look first, then name.** A Westfall "Harvest Watcher" is a scarecrow robot: the model says what it does.

## Rules for the Reach
1. Each zone: **4 families** (wildlife, local people, the story's enemy, the zone's oddity). See [[Bestiary]].
2. **Camps** of 4–8 linked mobs around a fire with one caster; a named leader at the zone's main camps.
3. **Patrols** on roads (pairs) and between camps.
4. **Levels rise with distance from the hub** across the zone's band.
5. **2–3 rares per zone**, 20–45 min respawn, each with a unique drop and a place you'd want to explore.
6. **Elite areas** only inside open-world dungeons and at the Ashen Scar.
7. Silhouette says behaviour: casters robed, brutes big (iron golem or ravager bodies), archers with bows.
