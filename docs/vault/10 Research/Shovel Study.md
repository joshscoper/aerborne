# Shovel (shovel241): what his fantasy cities do

Studied 2026-11-04 from his channel's world builds: "I Built for 700 Hours in Creative Mode" (a city stacked on a
giant flying turtle, the closest reference we have to a sky continent's capital), "I Built for 500 Hours" (a world
tree, a forest village and ruins), and "I'm Building the BEST Minecraft World of All Time". The captions are not
public without a signed token, so this comes from the videos' frame sheets and thumbnails. See [[Sources]].

## His process (shown on screen)
1. **A sketch first**: a blueprint drawing of each landmark's silhouette before any block is placed.
2. **Grey massing**: the shape in plain concrete, judged as a silhouette.
3. **Colour-blocking**: placeholder blocks (purple, gold, blue) mark where each material goes, before texture.
4. **Detail pass**: real materials, then props from a library.
5. **A library of parts**: brackets, beam ends, signs, crates and whole small structures, kept on a grid and reused
   all over the world, adjusted at every join.

## The city (the 700-hour build)
- **No flat ground is left over.** Buildings climb the slope in tiers, each set back and above the one in front, so
  roofs stack into one silhouette rising to the landmarks (a great gear-tower and a clock).
- **Every building sits on something**: a stone plinth, stilts or piles down to the rock, or a cantilever on
  diagonal struts. Nothing is half-buried. Where the ground falls away there are stilts; where it rises there's a
  retaining wall.
- **Stairs, walkways and bridges** join the levels, so the city reads as somewhere people walk.
- **A round plaza** with a radial stone pattern, stepped at its rim, as the city's centre.
- **Industry in the skyline**: chimneys, pipes, a water tower on stilts, cranes. They tell you what the city does.

## His houses
- **Deep eaves**, two or three blocks, carried on diagonal struts and brackets, with the shingle courses visible
  (stair rows and slab rows alternating).
- **Chunky timber**: frames two blocks thick at corners and floor lines, with beam ends poking through.
- **Big clean plaster panels** between the timbers, so the frame reads.
- **Wraparound balconies and galleries** on upper floors; jettied storeys stacked on one another.
- **Roof colour picks the district**: orange terracotta in one quarter, dark slate in another.
- **Windows recessed, framed and shuttered**; a lantern or sign at every door.

## Nature
- **Trees as heroes**: huge canopies made of many rounded clumps, with depth from light and shade, and roots
  spreading over the ground.
- **Planting tucked into the city**: vines, small trees in corners, flower boxes.

## What this means for the Reach
1. **Plan Highcrown as a stepped city.** Districts on terraces rise from the docks to the keep, and the skyline
   reads from the Vale and from the piers.
2. **Never place a building on raw ground.** Each one gets a graded site (cut and fill), a stone plinth, and a
   retaining wall or stilts where the ground differs from the floor. A test fails if a building overlaps the
   terrain.
3. **Roads are built things.** They need a surface, edges, verges, drainage, cuttings and embankments with walls,
   bridges, milestones and signposts, and lamps near towns.
4. **Give every street a purpose and a landmark at its end.** Markets, workshops, warehouses by the docks, a temple,
   guildhalls, the keep.
5. **Upgrade the house kit to his language**: deep eaves on struts, double timbers, balconies and coloured roofs by
   district.
6. **Props everywhere**, from a library: carts, crates, sacks, signs, laundry lines, flower boxes, barrels, benches,
   lamps.
7. **Bigger, clumpier tree canopies**, with roots.
